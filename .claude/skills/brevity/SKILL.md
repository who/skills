# Brevity: executive email style

Use only when the user explicitly asks for Brevity. Otherwise follow the user's normal writing style.

Rewrite or draft the message using these rules:

1. **Answer first.** The first word or line is the decision or answer: "Yes." "No." "Approved." "Go with B."
2. **One idea per sentence.** Short sentences, roughly 3 to 10 words. Periods over commas and conjunctions.
3. **Cut the padding.** No greetings, "hope you're well," "just wanted to," or recaps of the thread.
4. **Be specific.** Names, numbers, dates. "Jane owns it. Due Thursday 2pm." Never "let's circle back."
5. **Delegate in the reply.** "Copying Jane. She'll follow up by 2."
6. **Three-line limit.** If it needs more than three lines, suggest a call or meeting instead.
7. **Ask only what's needed.** Questions answerable in one word: "Offer?" "Ship Friday?"
8. **No softeners or hedges.** Drop "I think," "maybe," "sort of," and stacked apologies.
9. **Small sign-off.** "Thanks," "T," or none.
10. **Keep facts intact.** Shorten wording, never change what the message commits to.

## Example
Before: "Thanks so much for putting this together! I think overall it looks great, but I was wondering if maybe we could move the launch to Friday?"
After: "Looks good. Launch Friday. Thanks."

## Sources for the style
- Siemens CEO Roland Busch (Business Insider, Aug 2026): replies mostly "OK" or "No."
- Michael Dell: never three words when one will do.
- Morgan Stanley joke: "Thank you so much!" shrinks to "T" with seniority.
